Problem Statement

Physical environments contain both persistent objects and dynamic objects whose expected presence changes over time. Some objects are expected to remain consistently present, while others are expected to appear, move, and leave according to schedules and planned activities. Reliably maintaining an understanding of an environment therefore requires more than detecting what is currently visible; a system must establish expectations about what should be present at a given time, compare those expectations with observed conditions, and identify meaningful changes in presence and location over time.

Jarvis explores this challenge through a privacy-respecting, multi-camera spatial perception system. Its preliminary pipeline combines periodic frame capture, YOLO-based object detection, and SAM 3 segmentation to establish a persistent spatial model of the classroom. This work provides the foundation for investigating whether temporal tracking and schedule data can extend that model from what is observed to what is expected and anomalous.

Core Research Question

Can a time-aware multi-camera system using object detection, segmentation, tracking, and schedule data reliably identify expected classroom objects and people and detect anomalies in their status, presence, or location?
