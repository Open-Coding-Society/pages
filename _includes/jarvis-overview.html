Problem Statement

Managing and tracking classroom equipment, furniture arrangements, and shared physical assets in real-time is often manual, error-prone, and disconnected from active learning environments. Traditional computer vision applications in schools frequently focus on intrusive biometric identity scanning rather than ambient physical space awareness.

Jarvis addresses this challenge by deploying a privacy-respecting, multi-camera spatial perception pipeline. By combining high-definition periodic frame captures, YOLO-based bounding box detection, and SAM 3 pixel-level segmentation, the system maintains a persistent spatial model of the classroom without biometric identification.

Core Research Question

Can Jarvis use a low-cost, multi-camera computer-vision pipeline—combining timestamped classroom images, YOLO object recognition, and SAM 3 segmentation—to reliably identify classroom objects, determine where they are located, and track meaningful changes over time, while establishing a foundation for later material recognition?
