Problem Statement

Traditional classroom attendance systems typically rely on a single observation or event to determine whether a student is present. However, attendance does not necessarily represent sustained presence or the amount of time a student is available to participate in classroom learning and work. Many factors can affect the amount of time a student spends in the classroom, including tardiness, restroom breaks, counseling visits, excused absences, and other departures.

This project investigates how multiple independent presence signals can be correlated with classroom schedules and enrollment data to create a more accurate model of student presence over time. The resulting system will explore whether presence analytics can distinguish between attendance and actual time available for classroom engagement, providing students and educators with meaningful data about an individual's instructional access during the school day.

Research Question

Can a low-cost Raspberry Pi based UHF RFID system, using tamper-evident computer-mounted tags and doorway detection, reliably establish classroom device presence and contribute to student presence determination when correlated with an existing camera-spatial technology and class registration and bell-schedule data?

System Architecture

Two independent event sources, RFID and camera, feed a correlation engine that is also aware of the bell schedule and per-period enrollment.

This is the target architecture the team is building toward across four phases; the current prototype implements the leftmost slice of it.
