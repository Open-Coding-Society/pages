## From a project to a lasting system contribution

Use existing work to explain how your prior-year CSP community or nonprofit project experience prepared you to contribute to OCS. Identify what is now integrated into the shared system, what remains a prototype or proposal, and what future students can maintain or extend. A public demo does not by itself establish that a feature is integrated.

Point to existing interface contracts, integration tests, documentation, and known limitations. Explain how these make your contribution a foundation for future cohorts rather than a disposable sprint project. Record handoff gaps and next-step ideas; this retrospective does not require new implementation.

## Mentor and community review cycle

Review the team's **two mentor reviews and community N@tM presentation completed before week 13**. For each, identify the project discussed, the demonstrated behavior, and the explanation of **how it is made**. Link retained feedback and distinguish revisions already made from ideas for later iterations.

Explain your own role using existing design, code, integration, and test evidence. Group attendance, activity counts, and a teammate's implementation are not substitutes for individual explanation. This project/demo/technical-discussion cycle continues throughout the year.

## Choose a future topic and classroom contribution

Use the upcoming [CSA course plan]({{site.baseurl}}/csa/), [CS112 outline]({{site.baseurl}}/csa/cs112/), [CS113 outline]({{site.baseurl}}/csa/cs113/), and [Java Reference]({{site.baseurl}}/navigation/java-reference/) to select **one future learning topic** you want to help teach. Work individually or with **up to two other teammates**: two to three students total.

Record a short proposal in your existing review ticket:

- **Topic and timing:** Name the topic, link its existing lesson or outline section, and identify its planned teaching week when available. Choose upcoming instruction, not new work for an AP-review or presentation week.
- **Starting point:** Explain which current skills or project experience prepare you, and what you still need to learn. Selecting a topic is not claiming mastery.
- **Teaching contribution:** Propose a focused explanation, demonstration, guided activity, or CodeRunner exercise that helps classmates understand and practice the concept. Build on existing material rather than duplicate it.
- **System or classroom value:** Explain how the topic connects to your OCS feature or improves a reusable classroom learning resource.
- **Individual roles:** List the one to three participants and each person's proposed teaching or development contribution.
- **Feedback and coordination:** Bring the proposal to teacher review to coordinate topic coverage, preparation, and delivery in a later instructional week.

Week 12 is for choosing and proposing the contribution, not completing a new lesson, CodeRunner submission, or system implementation. Carry the reviewed proposal into CSA2 preparation; later teaching should include feedback and evidence of each participant's contribution.
