## Week 2: Design Specifications & Ethics

### Problem Identification
Draft a formal problem statement backed by statistical data, market research, or direct observation from affected stakeholders.

### Stakeholders
- List every stakeholder your solution affects — end users, teachers/administrators, mentors, or anyone else with a stake in the outcome.
- For each, note what they'd want out of your solution and any concerns they might raise.

### Design Criteria & Constraints
- Separate from your PRD's quantified metrics, list your **design criteria** (what a good solution must do) and **constraints** (time, budget, tools, skills) and **prioritize** them — not everything can be a top priority.

### Product Requirements Document (PRD)
Author a PRD defining strict, quantifiable target metrics, for example:
- System latency < 200ms
- Weight < 5 lbs
- Accuracy > 90%

### Ethics & Intellectual Property
Conduct a patent and open-source license search (MIT, GPL, Apache) to ensure your proposed solution does not violate existing IP.

### Peer Review (closes Week 2)
Trade your problem statement, literature review, and PRD draft with another
team.

### Problem Proposal Presentation
Close out Element C with a formal **Problem Proposal**: present your problem statement, justification evidence (Element A), similar solution matrix (Element B), stakeholders, and design criteria/constraints to your class or instructor.
- This is the checkpoint that greenlights moving into design work (Sprint 2) — treat it as a real proposal, not a status update.
- Incorporate peer-review feedback before presenting.

- Reviewers: check the problem statement is backed by evidence, the PRD
  metrics are quantifiable (not vague), and the literature review covers at
  least three prior-art sources with real limitations identified.
- Authors: log the feedback you received and note which points you'll revise
  before presenting to your mentor in Week 3.
