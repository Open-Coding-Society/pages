## Week 5: Design Concept Generation, Analysis & Selection

**PLTW Element D** — Design Concept Generation, Analysis, and Selection.

### Generate Concepts
- Sketch a **minimum of three** distinct design concepts that could satisfy your Sprint 1 PRD. Vary the concepts meaningfully — don't just re-skin the same architecture.
- Log each concept as its own GitHub Issue in your Kanban board, tagged `concept`, so the comparison trail is version-controlled instead of living on paper.

### Score Concepts in a Decision Matrix
- Build a weighted **Decision (Design) Matrix**: list your PRD criteria as rows, your concepts as columns, and score each concept against each criterion.
- Weight criteria by importance (e.g. feasibility, cost, learner/user impact, time-to-build) and total the scores to justify which concept you're carrying forward.

### Concept Testing
- Validate the leading concept cheaply before committing engineering time: a paper prototype, clickable wireframe, spike/proof-of-concept script, or structured stakeholder walkthrough.
- Record what the concept test told you — what changed, what got confirmed.

### My Solution
- Write up **My Solution**: the selected concept, in plain language, and **My Solution Justification** — why this concept won the decision matrix over the alternatives.

### First Mentor Meeting: Design Review
Early this sprint, present your decision matrix and selected concept to your industry mentor.
- Walk through why each losing concept was cut.
- Ask what a working engineer in this domain would flag as a risk in your leading concept.
- Log the mentor's feedback as a comment on your `concept` Issue — this becomes part of your Element J (External Evaluation) record later.

### Core Deliverables
- Decision Matrix with 3+ scored concepts.
- Concept test results.
- My Solution + My Solution Justification write-up.
- Logged mentor design-review notes.
